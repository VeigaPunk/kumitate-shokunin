## Cross-check: considerthese/2026-10-25 ideas 3–5

- **Idea 3, audit seat deaths:** directly matches dispatch/dead-seat recurrence (R60-V6, R61-V6, r62 Audit19, then Q59). The idea became a gate, but residual ambiguity and forged-host-record limits remain; audit from transcripts/dispatcher records rather than lane summary files.
- **Idea 4, count recurrences:** directly matches README-map recurrence and the new `routed-ledger`/`--classes` mechanism. This is valuable detection, not prevention: recurring classes should drive structural fixes; class labels/answers still need substantive human review. Round-63's tenth recurrence demonstrates why counts must trigger atomic workflow changes, not just another ledger row.
- **Idea 5, deferral ledger:** the 57-item corpus includes repeated carryovers (R61-V2 and Audit19, duplicate URL warnings, map drift), but these are defect/carryover records, not necessarily a normalized merge-candidate ledger with flip-condition state. A separate deferral ledger is complementary; it should not be conflated with routed defect recurrence.

## Bounded move

Pair the recurrence counter (Idea 4) with a close-time prevention gate for one high-frequency class: make every newly committed corpus Markdown file land atomically with its `README.md` row and `covered` pin, and re-run `readme-map` after close artifacts. This improves operability by preventing the repeatedly observed reference-drift instead of detecting it at the next plan; it need not weaken coverage or other gates. Risks: close-time integration ownership/write-scope coordination, and stale/misdescriptive rows remain possible because Q48 checks mapping presence, not row semantics. Verification: fixture mutants for missing row, missing pin, orphan row, duplicate row, and correct atomic landing; run against rounds 55–65 history and confirm historical recurrence is reported without retroactively changing verdicts.
